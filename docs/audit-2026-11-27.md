## 2. Findings that change what the map means

**A1. Four "restricted" entries are not restricted. EX.PRED, EX.LEV, TS.TYPED and TR.TYPED are equivalent to EX.FULL, TS.FULL and TR.FULL.** ISABELLE: `EX_PRED_iff_FULL`, `TS_TYPED_iff_FULL`, `TR_TYPED_iff_FULL`, and `EX_LEV_FULL` (EX.LEV gives EX.FULL for every admissible function that is a λ-term, which is all of them under C1, C2 and C3d). The cause is in decision D3's wording: a restriction "applies to a proposition whenever some formula with the property denotes it under some environment". The formula `Var 0` has level 0 and is truth-free, and under the environment sending 0 to q it denotes q, so every proposition meets every such restriction. Consequences:

- Krämer's own escape route does not escape in this encoding: `[C1, BETA.ID, CL.QUANT, EX.PRED, IRR.F]` is refuted (ISABELLE: `kramer_not_blocked_by_EX_PRED`). Krämer offers the restriction of (EG-S) to quantifier-free α as a way of rejecting (X) (FETCH).
- The registry's remark under TS.TYPED that "Fine-style circles are already blocked by TR.TYPED" is false of the encoding.
- The section 4 certificates for EX.PRED, EX.LEV, TS.TYPED and TR.TYPED are certificates for the unrestricted principles. They remain true; they are uninformative about the restricted ones.
- Every search result that mentions one of the four entries should be read with the unrestricted entry in its place.

What would falsify A1: nothing short of an error in Isabelle; the lemmas are about the definitions in `Principles.thy` as they stand. Proposed repair, for the reviewer to decide: quantify the restricted entries over closed formulas (`fv a = {}`), so that "quantifier-free" and "truth-free" describe sentences, as Krämer's α and Korbmacher's Sent do. That changes D3's convention and needs a registry version bump; the four entries then need new models and new queries. The same question arises for C2, whose level bound does not bind parameters (INFERENCE: a λ-term of level 0 may carry a proposition of any level as the value of a free variable, so C2 is predicative in its bound variable only).

**A2. The "chain" convention in the registry header is not what Isabelle implements.** The header says that choosing an entry of a chain includes every weaker entry and that statements are cumulative unless marked standalone. `Principles.thy` defines every entry on its own, and the search reports packages by entry id. So a reader who follows the registry will take `CL.QUANT` in a result to include `CL.PROP`, `ID.DN` to include `ID.AC`, `CONJ.INF` and `CONJ.GRAIN` to include `CONJ.BIN`, `DISJ.BOTH` to include `DISJ.ONE`, `NEG.DM` to include `NEG.DN`, and `TR.NEG` to include `TR.FULL`. None of these inclusions holds in the encoding, and none is a provable implication (`Order.thy` proves only the ones listed in section 5 below). Example from the results page: the minimal refuted package `CL.PROP, IRR.F, TR.NEG, TRANS.CUT, TS.ID` contains the falsity clause of the Aristotelian principle without TR.FULL. `Order.thy` already says rank numbers are not used; the registry header and the per-entry remarks ("DN includes AC", "added on top of TR.FULL") say the opposite. Recommendation: make the registry say what the code does, namely that entries are standalone, `rank` is a label, and the only order is the implications proved in `Order.thy`.

**A3. "trivial-only" is assigned without a model, and its gloss claims consistency.** `scripts/driver.py` assigns trivial-only when Sledgehammer proves `P ∧ N1 ⟹ False` and has not proved `P ⟹ False`. The results page glosses the class as "consistent only in models where nothing grounds anything". That is a consistency claim resting on a failed proof search, which the project's own rule forbids. It is also false in at least one listed case: `BETA.ID, C1, CL.QUANT, EX.FULL, IRR.F` is listed as trivial-only in section 2 of the results page and is refuted outright (ISABELLE: `kramer_target_as_listed`). The reason is that the third clause of CL.QUANT makes the conjunction of an empty satisfier set true, so CL.QUANT alone yields a truth (ISABELLE: `CL_QUANT_truth`). Recommendation: rename the class to what was shown ("refuted with N1; plain refutation not found"), or require a checked model of `P ∧ ¬N1` before using the present gloss. The two other trivial-only rows that contain CL.QUANT should be re-queried.

**A4. Fine's Universal Grounding has no entry, and the two ALL entries are not alternatives with respect to his puzzle.** Fine 2010 states Universal Grounding for partial ground: each instance helps ground the universal truth (p. 100, PAGE). ALL.INST (all instances are a full ground) and ALL.TOT (instances plus a totality fact) each imply it (ISABELLE: `ALL_INST_PART`, `ALL_TOT_PART`), and the Universal Argument for Propositions goes through from partial-ground premises alone (ISABELLE: `fine_universal_partial`) and therefore with ALL.TOT in place of ALL.INST (ISABELLE: `fine_universal_with_ALL_TOT`). The registry lists the two as mutually exclusive alternatives and gives the puzzle's package with ALL.INST, so the map will overstate what the puzzle needs. Fine makes the point himself: the problem of the absolute full ground of a universal truth "need not be solved in order to pose the puzzle" (p. 109, PAGE). Recommendation: add the partial principle as the weakest ALL entry and state Fine's package with it. The same holds for his Disjunctive, Existential and Propositional Grounding, all partial in 2010 (pp. 99–101, PAGE); DISJ.WEAK and EX.WEAK are not those principles.

**A5. Fine's Cut is misquoted.** The registry quotes "Cut(</<)" with a strict major premise and strict conclusion. The page shows Cut(≤/≤), weak throughout (Fine 2012b p. 5, PAGE; the PDF's text layer is OCR and confuses the four symbols). TRANS.CUT is therefore not a special case of Fine's primitive rule, as the entry said. It is a derived rule of PLG (INFERENCE: Subsumption, Identity and Cut give the weak sequent, Subsumption(</≺) and Transitivity(≺/≼) make each member a strict partial ground, Reverse Subsumption gives the strict sequent; Fine derives Amalgamation(<) on p. 7 in the same way). Quote and note corrected in this pull request; the statement is unchanged.

## 9. The four certificate rules: a second opinion

These are recommendations. The reviewer is settling the same questions in the model-certificates thread; nothing here has been applied.

**Rule 1. May a model checked only by Isabelle be labelled "finite-model" without the Python re-check?** Yes, with the kind of certificate recorded. The Python re-check exists because Nitpick's output is not a proof: it guards against a spurious or mistranslated counter-model. A locale interpretation is checked by the Isabelle kernel, so that risk is absent. What neither route guards against is a mismatch between what was checked and what the registry says. For the Isabelle route that gap is closed inside Isabelle by `K_faithful` (the locale's definitions equal those of `Principles.thy`), and remains open between `Principles.thy` and the registry text, as finding A1 shows. Giving the Python checker a formula layer is possible in principle for finite carriers (the denotations of formulas form a finite set that can be computed by closure) and would be a second implementation of the same definitions; it would not have caught A1, because it would reproduce the same convention. Recommendation: one label, and a field `certificate: nitpick+independent-check | isabelle-interpretation` shown on the results page.

**Rule 2. Does N3 count as met when the package has no introduction principle?** Logically yes: N3 quantifies over the selected introduction entries. As a reported level it misleads, because N3 is cumulative and N4 and N5 are then advertised on a base that tested nothing. Recommendation: report "N3 not applicable" and keep N4 and N5 as separate columns for such packages. Also extend N3's list: (∧1) of IMM.FRITZ7 and BETA.GROUND are introduction principles in content and are not on it.

**Rule 3. May N4 be stated with an explicit pair?** An explicit witness is stronger than an existential, so there is no loss. Two things need tightening. The certificate states `N4_pair P1 (nx (nx P1))`, and that this pair has the form (p, ¬¬p) is visible only by reading the interpretation, where negation is the successor on a five-element cycle; the theorem should state the form (∃p. N4_pair p (neg (neg p)), and so on) so that the kernel checks it. And N4's own reading is "pairs that are classically equivalent by construction". In the five-proposition models every proposition is true, p and ¬p included, so the pair is equivalent in no sense the reading intends. Recommendation: N4 counts only in a model that satisfies CL.PROP; otherwise report it as "N4 (formal)".

**Rule 4. How thin may a model be?** The five-proposition models certify exactly this: the principle, with factivity, is satisfiable with some ground, three truths and a chain of four propositions, in a structure where every proposition is true, conjunction and disjunction are constant, and there is one propositional function. That is a legitimate answer to "is this principle consistent on its own" and no answer to "is it consistent with classical truth". "Classical" should mean: the model satisfies CL.PROP, and CL.QUANT when quantifiers are present. In that sense the IMM.FRITZ7 model ME is not classical: negations and disjunctions are never true, so the negation and disjunction clauses of CL.PROP fail. Recommendation: (i) keep the thin certificates, each with a model profile (classical or not, carrier sizes, which connectives are constant); (ii) make "principle plus CL.PROP (plus CL.QUANT)" a standard query, reported separately, since that is the baseline the literature assumes; (iii) treat IMM.FRITZ7 with CL.PROP and without C3s as an open target and say so wherever F2 is cited; (iv) re-issue the certificates for EX.PRED, EX.LEV, TS.TYPED and TR.TYPED after A1 is repaired.
